Appendix A of the paper

The expansions for any \(d\) and order \(M\)

Let \(R_j=X_j/Y_j\), \(j=1,\ldots,d\), with \(Y_j>0\) and joint cumulant generating function \(\mathcal K(s,t)\) of \((X,Y)\), and let \(W_j=X_j-r_jY_j\), whose cumulant generating function is \(K_W(s)=\mathcal K(s,-r\circ s)\). As in the applications of the paper, \(n\) is absorbed into \(\mathcal K\). The distribution function \(\mathrm{P}(R\leq r)=\mathrm{P}(-W\geq0)\) is the orthant probability of \(-W\) at \(0\), given by Theorem 1 with \(x=0\) and by (17) when the saddlepoint has negative components. The density is the saddlepoint integral (45), whose amplitude collects the derivatives \(\mathcal K_B=\partial^{|B|}\mathcal K/\prod_{j\in B}\partial t_j\) over the partitions \(\pi\) of \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\):

\[ f_R(r)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{d}}\int_{\hat s-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\hat s+\mathrm{i}\infty}e^{K_W(s)}g(s)\,ds,\qquad g=\sum_{\pi}\prod_{B\in\pi}\mathcal K_B(s,-r\circ s)=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}g_k, \] \[ f_R(r)\approx\frac{e^{K_W(\hat s)}}{(2\pi)^{d/2}|K_W''(\hat s)|^{1/2}}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\tilde a_m,\qquad \tilde a_m=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m,d-1)}b_{m-k}(g_k), \] \[ b_m(h)=\sum_{r=0}^{2m}\ \sum_{j=3r}^{2m+2r}\ \sum_{|\nu|=j}\ \sum_{|\mu|=2m+2r-j}\tilde c_{r,\nu}\,\gamma_\mu\,\mathrm{He}_{\nu+\mu}(0),\qquad \tilde c_{0,0}=1, \]

Here \(K_W'(\hat s)=0\), \(g_k\) collects the partitions into \(d-k\) blocks, and everything is whitened as on the companion page for \(d\) random variables: with \(K_W''(\hat s)=LL^{\top}\), \(\theta_\nu\) are the Taylor coefficients of \(K_W(L^{-\top}u)\) at \(\hat u=L^{\top}\hat s\) (\(|\nu|\geq3\)), the \(\tilde c_{r,\nu}\) follow from the recursion (5) applied to the \(\theta_\nu\), \(\gamma_\mu\) are the Taylor coefficients of \(h(L^{-\top}u)\) at \(\hat u\) (the \(\gamma_\mu\) of Theorem 4 in the whitened coordinates), and \(\mathrm{He}_\nu(0)=\prod_j\mathrm{He}_{\nu_j}(0)\). This is Theorem 4 and Corollary 3. For \(d=1\), \(b_m(g)/\gamma_0\) is the coefficient \(\tilde a_m\) of Rice's expansion (Corollary 5), for example \(\tilde a_1=3\theta_4-\tfrac{15}{2}\theta_3^2+3\theta_3\tilde\gamma_1-\tilde\gamma_2\) with \(\tilde\gamma_l=\gamma_l/\gamma_0\).

Example 1 · \(d=1\)

A ratio of quadratic forms

\(R=z^{\top}Az/z^{\top}Bz\) with \(B\) positive definite and \(z\sim N(\mu,\Sigma)\) in \(\mathbb{R}^N\), where \(N\) plays the role of \(n\) (set it below; the default is \(N=30\)). The point is \(r=r_0+q\,s_0\), with \(r_0=\operatorname{E}(z^{\top}Az)/\operatorname{E}(z^{\top}Bz)\) and \(s_0=\operatorname{sd}(W)/\operatorname{E}(z^{\top}Bz)\) at \(r_0\). With \(\Sigma=RR^{\top}\), \(m=R^{-1}\mu\), \(\tilde A=R^{\top}AR\), \(\tilde B=R^{\top}BR\) and \(D=\{I-2s(\tilde A-r\tilde B)\}^{-1}\):

\[ K_W(s)=-\tfrac12\log|D^{-1}|+\tfrac12m^{\top}(D-I)m,\qquad g(s)=\mathcal K_t(s,-rs)=\operatorname{tr}(D\tilde B)+m^{\top}D\tilde BDm. \]

The density is Rice's expansion (Corollary 5) and the distribution function is the Hauschildt–Robinson expansion applied to \(W\) at \(0\) (Corollary 4). The Taylor coefficients of \(g\) are the mixed derivatives of the cumulant generating function of the forms with matrices \(\tilde A-r\tilde B\) and \(\tilde B\), computed by the trace recursion of the companion page.

Example 2 · \(d=2\) or \(3\)

Sample autocorrelations

As in Section S4 of the Supplementary Material, \(z\sim N(0,\Sigma)\) with \(\Sigma_{ts}=\rho^{|t-s|}\) holds \(N\) observations of a first-order autoregression, and \(R_j=z^{\top}MA_jMz/z^{\top}Mz\), \(j=1,\ldots,d\), are the sample autocorrelations at lags \(1,\ldots,d\), with \(M=I-1_N1_N^{\top}/N\) (or \(M=I\) without the mean correction) and \((A_j)_{ts}=\tfrac12\) for \(|t-s|=j\). The common denominator makes \(\mathcal K\) depend on \(t\) only through \(t_1+\cdots+t_d\), so that every \(\mathcal K_B\) is a derivative of order \(|B|\) in that direction. The points are \(r_j=\rho^j+q_jN^{-1/2}\).

Terms of the distribution function

Exact values: numerical inversion of (45) for the density and of the orthant probability of \(-W\) for the distribution function, along a contour through the saddlepoint (moved just right of the poles when a component of the saddlepoint is near zero), with (17) applied exactly. In whitened polar coordinates each ray reduces to a real symmetric eigenproblem; the amplitude \(g\) then costs \(O(N)\) per radial node for one ratio and \(O(N^2)\) for two. For three ratios the amplitude needs \(O(N^3)\) per node, which is too slow for a browser, so the exact density is not computed for \(d=3\); the exact distribution function is computed with a time limit of about 30 seconds and its accuracy is shown. The rays are shared among several background workers, one per processor core. For \(N=50\) and \(100\) the code reproduces the relative errors of Table S8 of the Supplementary Material, whose exact values were computed independently.
Matrices for Example 1: \(A\), \(B\) and \(\Sigma\) are \(N\times N\) Toeplitz matrices \(T_N(\rho)\) with \((i,k)\) entry \(\rho^{|i-k|}\) (\(T_N(0)=I_N\)), and \(\mu=c\,1_N\). Relative errors: density \((\hat f-f)/f\); distribution function \((\hat F-F)/\min(F,1-F)\). Everything runs in your browser in double precision.
